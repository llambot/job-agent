\opening{\vspace{12mm}Dear Hiring Manager,}

\vspace{5mm}I am writing to express my strong interest in the scientific and technical advisor position at Intertwine Associates. With a PhD from ULB Faculty of Medicine, 15 years of research experience spanning computational neuroscience and biomedical engineering, and proven success translating research into practical applications, I bring the breadth of expertise, strategic thinking, and stakeholder communication skills this role demands.

My background uniquely positions me to evaluate and shape biomedical R\&D programs. I completed five years of medical and biomedical school training at UCL Faculty of Medicine and a PhD at ULB Faculty of Medicine in Belgium, followed by postdoctoral positions at Northwestern's Feinberg School of Medicine and University of Chicago. This medical and biomedicale foundation, combined with computational expertise in Python, MATLAB, and machine learning applications, allows me to assess technologies from both clinical and technical perspectives—a critical skill for advising on programs spanning diagnostics, biosensors, and integrated biomedical platforms.

At Modendo, I drove innovation from concept through market engagement. I developed wave-front shaping ultra-thin endoscopes for minimally invasive deep-brain imaging, designed ISO-compliant biocompatible cranial implants, and manufactured optical imaging tools (\href{https://lambot.co/project/chapter5/}{\underline{\textbf{see details}}} \href{https://lambot.co/project/chapter5/}{\underline{\textbf{here}}}). Beyond technical development, I represented the company at major conferences (BRAIN Initiative, NINDS, Society for Neuroscience), engaged directly with end users to refine product-market fit, and pitched to investors through the Ascent Deep Tech Accelerator. This work required synthesizing technical feasibility, clinical utility, regulatory pathways, and market potential—exactly the strategic assessment this role requires.

My research track record demonstrates the technical depth and innovation capacity essential for this position. I independently designed and built custom imaging platforms that captured activity from 400+ neurons simultaneously while recording multimodal behavioral data—inte- grating CAD design, 3D printing, system engineering, and programming in MATLAB, Python, and C++ (\href{https://lambot.co/project/chapter2/}{\underline{\textbf{see full technical details}}} \href{https://lambot.co/project/chapter2/}{\underline{\textbf{here}}}). This required mastering system integration, synchronization of multiple data streams with sub-millisecond precision, and developing machine learning pipelines for real-time analysis. These technical capabilities—building complex experimental platforms from scratch, integrating diverse technologies, and solving multidisciplinary engineering challenges—directly translate to evaluating and shaping biomedical R\&D programs.

I have extensive experience with computational modeling, systems integration, and AI/ML applications in biomedical contexts. At University of Chicago, I built generalized linear models to predict neural activity from multiple physiological signals and implemented machine learning tools like DeepLabCut for behavioral analysis. I designed and engineered custom experimental platforms from the ground up—integrating two-photon imaging with high-speed cameras, rotary encoders, pupilometry, and Arduino-controlled actuators. All components were synchronized with sub-millisecond precision, requiring expertise in hardware integration, signal processing, and real-time data management. I managed terabytes of imaging data and developed custom algorithms for neural classification, demonstrating capabilities directly relevant to assessing emerging integrated biomedical platforms.

My collaborative research with Janssen Pharmaceuticals on Alzheimer's disease therapeutics strengthened my ability to work across industry and academic contexts, evaluate drug development strategies, and communicate with diverse stakeholders ranging from bench scientists to pharmaceutical executives. I presented findings to Janssen R\&D leadership and European neuroscience leaders, developed protocols for isolating transplanted human neurons, and contributed to work that advanced toward clinical trials. This experience taught me to assess technologies through multiple lenses—scientific validity, clinical translation potential, regulatory pathways, and commercial viability. This collaboration resulted in a publication in \textit{Neuron}, and my broader research contributions include publications in \textit{Nature Neuroscience} and the \textit{Journal of Neuroscience}.
